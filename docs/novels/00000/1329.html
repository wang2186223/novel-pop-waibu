Ten years had sharpened the lines of his face, carved deeper shadows into his jaw, and solidified the predatory stillness that had once captivated her—before he dismantled her life with a single, calculated decision.

He didn't move toward her. He stood with his back to the mirrored wall, his eyes tracking her with the same intensity he applied to corporate acquisitions. The air between them grew heavy, charged with the static electricity of a decade-old debt. Mirella adjusted her glasses, the blue light of the emergency panel reflecting off the lenses to hide her eyes.

"The building’s grid is failing, Barrett. Even your influence can't override the physics of a power failure."

Barrett stepped forward, though he kept a respectful, tense distance. He occupied the space like he owned it, his presence expanding to fill the small steel box. "I don't recall asking for a structural analysis. I recall a woman who vanished the moment the Croft Holdings audit ended. Strange, how you walked away from the most lucrative portfolio in the city only to resurface as a low-level consultant for a boutique firm."

Mirella felt the familiar tightening in her chest. Every word he spoke was a probe, a surgical incision meant to expose the secret she guarded.
