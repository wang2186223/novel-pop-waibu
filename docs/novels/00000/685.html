“I’m just curious, Harlow. You have a peculiar habit of lingering near the CFO’s private files every time you clean this wing.”

She stopped, the mop handle clattering against the bucket. She turned, meeting his gaze. She felt a jolt of electricity ripple through her, a mix of defensive pride and an inexplicable, annoying curiosity about who he really was.

She dropped her facade, slipped a drive into the hidden port behind the CFO’s mahogany desk, and watched the progress bar crawl toward completion. Every executive seemed to be watching, their eyes darting toward the janitorial staff with newfound suspicion. Harlow felt the walls closing in. Rylan was everywhere. He was in the breakroom when she went for water, his gaze tracking her every move. He was near the printers, intercepting documents that belonged in the shredder. The tension between them had reached a breaking point, a silent war of glares and clipped, biting sentences.

“You’re getting sloppy,” Rylan muttered one Tuesday morning as she pushed her heavy cart past his desk. He was leaning back, tapping a pen against his chin. “They know someone is inside the system, Harlow. They’re looking for a scapegoat.”

“You’re leaving a trail of breadcrumbs that leads directly to your locker,” he countered. His voice wasn't a taunt anymore; it was a warning. His steel-gray eyes were filled with an urgency that unsettled her. “You think you’re hidden, but you’re standing in the middle of a spotlight you can’t even see.”

“If you’re so worried about me, why don't you report me?” She stopped, leaning over his desk, her face inches from his. The proximity was suffocating. “Go ahead. Tell them the janitor is stealing their secrets. See what happens to your little internship then.”
